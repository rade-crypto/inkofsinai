Barren women in the Bible

Who they were, where the Hebrew says it, and the son each one had

By Ink of SinaiChecked against the Hebrew textHow we checkPublished November 8, 2026
Short answer

The Hebrew word for barren, עֲקָרָה (akarah), is used of four women in the Bible's stories: Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Samson's mother. Two more wait years for a son: Hannah, whose womb “was closed,” and the Shunammite woman, who “has no son.” Each of the six later has a son.

Hannah is never called עֲקָרָה, but her song uses the word: עַד־עֲקָרָה יָלְדָה שִׁבְעָה, “the barren woman has borne seven” (1 Samuel 2:5).

What the six stories share

In none of them is the birth ordinary. Each son comes after a promise, a prayer, or a visit, and the text says so in each case (the third column above). Several of the sons are the ones the story then follows: Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Samson and Samuel.

Questions people ask

Who were the barren women in the Bible?

Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Samson's mother are called barren. Hannah and the Shunammite woman also wait for a son.

What is the Hebrew word for barren?

עֲקָרָה (akarah).

Why were so many women in the Bible barren?

The text doesn't give one reason. In each story the son comes through a promise, a prayer or a visit, which the text records each time.

Which barren woman had seven children?

None in the stories. “The barren woman has borne seven” is a line in Hannah's song (1 Samuel 2:5).
