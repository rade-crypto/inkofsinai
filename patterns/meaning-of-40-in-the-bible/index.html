What does the number 40 mean in the Bible?

The 40 days and 40 years of the Hebrew Bible, verse by verse

By Ink of SinaiChecked against the Hebrew textHow we checkPublished November 8, 2026
Short answer

In the Hebrew Bible, 40 days and 40 years mark a set time of waiting or testing before something new begins: 40 days of rain (Genesis 7:12), 40 days for Moses on the mountain (Exodus 24:18), 40 days of scouting the land (Numbers 13:25), 40 years in the wilderness (Numbers 14:33), and 40 days for Nineveh to turn (Jonah 3:4). Deuteronomy says what the 40 wilderness years were for: “to test you” (Deuteronomy 8:2).

What the text says 40 is for

Moses tells Israel to remember the whole way they were led זֶה אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה, “these forty years,” in the wilderness, לְנַסֹּתְךָ, “to test you,” to know what was in their heart (Deuteronomy 8:2). The 40 days Moses spends on the mountain end with the tablets. The 40 days of rain end with a new start for the earth. Jonah gives Nineveh 40 days, and the city turns.

Questions people ask

What does 40 symbolize in the Bible?

A set time of waiting or testing before something new. Deuteronomy calls the 40 wilderness years a test (Deuteronomy 8:2).

What happened for 40 days in the Bible?

The flood rain, Moses on the mountain (twice), the spies in Canaan, Goliath's challenge, Elijah's walk to Horeb, Ezekiel's sign and Nineveh's warning, all listed above.

Why did Israel wander for 40 years?

After the spies' report, the people refused to enter the land, and the wilderness years were set at one year for each of the 40 days of scouting (Numbers 14:34).

Who reigned for 40 years in the Bible?

David (2 Samuel 5:4) and Solomon (1 Kings 11:42).
