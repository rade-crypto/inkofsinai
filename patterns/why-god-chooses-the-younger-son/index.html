Why does God choose the younger son?

The younger brothers who take the firstborn's place, with the verses

By Ink of SinaiChecked against the Hebrew textHow we checkPublished November 8, 2026
Short answer

Again and again in the Bible the younger brother is chosen over the older: Abel over Cain, Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Ephraim over Manasseh, and later David, the youngest of Jesse's sons. The Torah's own law gives the firstborn a double share (Deuteronomy 21:17), so each choice goes against the expected order. When David is chosen, the text gives a reason: people look at what the eyes see, but God looks at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7).

Against the law of the firstborn

The Torah protects the firstborn: even if a man loves another wife more, he must give his firstborn פִּי שְׁנַיִם, “a double portion” (Deuteronomy 21:17). So when the younger son keeps winning in the stories, it stands out.

The reason the text gives

When Samuel is sent to anoint one of Jesse's sons, he expects the tall eldest, Eliab. He is told not to look at his height: people look at what the eyes see, but God looks at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). Then Jesse admits there is one more son, out with the sheep (1 Samuel 16:11).

Jacob blesses Ephraim over Manasseh in the Torah portion Vayechi, read this year on December 26.

Questions people ask

Why does God choose the younger son?

The text gives its reason when David is chosen: people look at appearance, but God looks at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7).

Who was chosen over their older brother in the Bible?

Abel, Isaac, Jacob, Perez, Ephraim, Moses, David and Solomon, listed above with the verses.

Why did Jacob bless Ephraim over Manasseh?

Jacob says the younger brother will be greater than the older (Genesis 48:19).

What does the Bible say about the firstborn?

The firstborn gets a double portion of the inheritance (Deuteronomy 21:17).
