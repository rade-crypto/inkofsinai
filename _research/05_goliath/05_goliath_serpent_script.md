# 05 · Why the Bible Dresses Goliath Like a Serpent. Voiceover, draft 7 (for review)

**Title:** Why the Bible Dresses Goliath Like a Serpent
**Thumbnail:** `_share/05_goliath_thumbnail.jpg`: Goliath in gold ink, a glowing circle on his coat of scales, with "LOOK AT / HIS ARMOR."

---

Before Goliath says a single word, the Bible spends three verses on what he's wearing.

Almost all of it is bronze. In Hebrew, bronze is נְחֹשֶׁת. The serpent in Eden is נָחָשׁ. Say them together, and you can hear the serpent inside the bronze.

The guards on his legs are bronze too. Their Hebrew name comes from the word for forehead. A "forehead" on his legs. You won't find that word anywhere else in the Bible.

There are four clues like that in this chapter, and the third one points to the spot where the stone lands.

Clue one: count the bronze. A bronze helmet. A coat of five thousand shekels of bronze. Bronze on his legs. A bronze javelin. Only his spearhead is iron.

Bronze is an ordinary Hebrew word. But four times in two verses, on one man, is a pattern.

And the Bible puts serpent and bronze together itself. In the desert, when serpents bite the people, Moses makes נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת, a serpent of bronze.

The next piece of Goliath's armor points to something bigger than a snake.

Clue two is on his chest. His armor is made of scales. In Hebrew, קַשְׂקַשִּׂים.

Most of the time, that word is on fish. But one other creature wears it: a dragon. The prophet Ezekiel calls Pharaoh a great dragon lying in his river, and he uses this same word for the dragon's scales.

So the giant walks out dressed as a serpent and a dragon. And back in Genesis 3, the first serpent is told where it will be hit.

After the serpent tricks Eve, God sentences it. It will crawl on its belly and eat dust. And the woman's children will fight it: הוּא יְשׁוּפְךָ רֹאשׁ, "they will strike your head."

The head is about to show up in the valley.

But the giant can't get a fight. For אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם, forty days, he walks out morning and evening and dares Israel to send one man. And for forty days, nobody goes. When Israel's soldiers see him, they run.

Then a shepherd boy shows up with bread for his brothers, and he says he'll fight. He's already killed a lion and a bear. He grabbed one by its beard. Neither of them wore bronze down to the shins.

Clue three is that bronze "forehead" on his legs, מִצְחַת נְחֹשֶׁת. He has a bronze helmet on his head, and a bronze "forehead" down on his shins.

Now watch where the stone goes.

David reaches into his bag, slings one stone, and the narrator slows down: "He struck the Philistine אֶל מִצְחוֹ, on his forehead. The stone sank into his forehead." The narrator says מִצְחוֹ twice. It's the forehead word from his legs, and this time it's his real forehead.

Then, וַיִּפֹּל עַל פָּנָיו אָרְצָה, "he fell on his face to the ground." The serpent was sentenced to crawl in the dust. The giant ends face-down on the ground.

David runs, draws Goliath's own sword, and cuts off his head, the spot Genesis named.

And the Philistines have seen this before. Earlier in the same book, they capture Israel's ark and set it in the temple of their god, Dagon. The next morning, Dagon himself is lying נֹפֵל לְפָנָיו אַרְצָה, "fallen on his face to the ground," in front of the ark. The morning after, he's on his face again, and his head has been cut off.

And clue four is on David.

Before the fight, someone tried to put bronze on him: King Saul.

He's the tallest man in Israel. But when Goliath shouts his challenge, the Bible says he and all Israel were "dismayed and terribly afraid."

So he dresses David in his own garments and sets קוֹבַע נְחֹשֶׁת עַל רֹאשׁוֹ, "a helmet of bronze on his head."

Those are almost the same words the Bible used for Goliath's helmet.

David takes a few steps and says, "I can't walk in these." Then, וַיְסִרֵם דָּוִד מֵעָלָיו, "David removed them from upon himself."

Then that same phrase comes out of David's own mouth, when he faces the giant: וַהֲסִרֹתִי אֶת רֹאשְׁךָ מֵעָלֶיךָ, "I will remove your head from upon you."
He walks out with a staff, five smooth stones and a sling. There's no bronze anywhere on him.

The giant looks at the boy with contempt. "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks? Come here, and I'll give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field."

David answers, "You come to me with sword and spear and javelin, and I come to you in the name of God."

And the narrator adds one line, וְחֶרֶב אֵין בְּיַד דָּוִד, "and there was no sword in David's hand."

The only sword in the fight was Goliath's, and it took his head.

And the giant isn't the only one who falls.

When the Philistines see their champion dead, they run. For forty days Goliath stood in that valley, and Israel ran from him. Now Israel rises with a shout and chases them. Philistine dead fall along the road as far as Gath, Goliath's hometown.

David had said this would happen. Before the fight, he told the giant, "Today I will give the bodies of the Philistine army to the birds of the sky and the wild animals of the earth." It was the giant's own threat, turned around on his whole army. And he said why: וְיֵדְעוּ כָּל־הָאָרֶץ, "so the whole earth will know that there is a God in Israel."

So look at what the earth got to see. A giant dressed as the serpent, in five thousand shekels of bronze. And against him, a shepherd boy with a stone from a stream.

And before that stone ever left his sling, the boy told everyone whose win it would be:

"God does not save with sword and spear, for the battle belongs to God."

## Checked in the Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex)
- **Armor and bronze:**
  - Goliath's armor takes three verses (1 Sam 17:5–7).
  - נְחֹשֶׁת appears four times in 17:5–6. The spearhead is iron (17:7).
  - נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת (Numbers 21:9); נְחֻשְׁתָּן (2 Kings 18:4).
- **Scales, dragon, serpent:**
  - קַשְׂקַשִּׂים appears only in Leviticus 11:9, 10, 12; Deuteronomy 14:9, 10; 1 Samuel 17:5; and Ezekiel 29:4, where Pharaoh is הַתַּנִּים הַגָּדוֹל (29:3).
- **Genesis 3:**
  - Genesis 3:14 (עַל גְּחֹנְךָ תֵלֵךְ וְעָפָר תֹּאכַל) and 3:15 (הוּא יְשׁוּפְךָ רֹאשׁ). The "he" there is the woman's offspring, so it's rendered "they."
  - Samuel's verbs (וַיַּךְ, וַיִּכְרֹת) differ from Genesis. The shared word is רֹאשׁ.
- **The forehead:**
  - מִצְחַת appears only at 1 Samuel 17:6.
  - מִצְחוֹ appears twice in 17:49, along with עַל פָּנָיו אָרְצָה.
  - The bronze helmet is in 17:5.
- **Saul:**
  - Tallest in Israel (9:2; 10:23); afraid (17:11).
  - 17:38 nearly repeats 17:5, with כּוֹבַע / קוֹבַע.
- **"Remove … from upon":**
  - הֵסִיר … מֵעַל appears three times: 17:26 (David's first quoted words; 17:22 only reports his speech), 17:39 and 17:46.
- **No sword:** "No sword in David's hand" (17:50). Goliath's sword takes his head (17:51).
- **The ending:**
  - Goliath stood forty days (17:16), and Israel fled from him (17:24).
  - The Philistines see that גִּבּוֹרָם, their champion, is dead and flee (17:51).
  - Israel shouts and pursues them (17:52). The Philistine dead fall "on the road… as far as Gath and Ekron" (17:52), and Goliath is from Gath (17:4).
  - Five smooth stones (17:40); one stone (17:49).
  - וְיֵדְעוּ כָּל הָאָרֶץ כִּי יֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים לְיִשְׂרָאֵל (17:46).
  - Closing verse 17:47, the last line of David's speech before he runs (17:48). On screen, quote it with ellipses, since it's a partial verse.
- **New beats (draft 5):**
  - Forty days, morning and evening, "took his stand" (17:16). The challenge is 17:8–10.
  - Israel flees from him (17:24). David brings grain and bread for his brothers (17:17).
  - "I'll go and fight him" (17:32). "You're a boy… a warrior since his youth" (17:33).
  - The lion and the bear, "by its beard," "like one of them" (17:34–36). 17:37 has the Divine Name, so it isn't quoted.
  - Goliath's contempt and "Am I a dog… with sticks?" (17:42–43). "Your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field" (17:44), turned around in 17:46.
  - Dagon: brought into Dagon's temple (5:2). נֹפֵל לְפָנָיו אַרְצָה (5:3, 5:4). Head and hands כְּרֻתוֹת, "cut off" (5:4). Compare וַיִּפֹּל עַל פָּנָיו אָרְצָה (17:49) and וַיִּכְרָת… אֶת רֹאשׁוֹ (17:51). On screen, show only נֹפֵל לְפָנָיו אַרְצָה, because the rest of the verse has the Divine Name.
- **On screen:** 17:45 and 17:47 in English only, because their Hebrew contains the Divine Name. For 17:46, show only וְיֵדְעוּ כָּל הָאָרֶץ.
- **The scales word:** the exact form קַשְׂקַשִּׂים appears only at 17:5. The same word, as קַשְׂקֶשֶׂת or קַשְׂקְשֹׂת, appears only in Leviticus 11, Deuteronomy 14 and Ezekiel 29:4.
