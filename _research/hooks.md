# Hooks playbook (researched Nov 3, 2026)

Read this before writing any hook, title or first line. The full notes with sources and quotes are in the session scratchpad (hookresearch/classic_notes.md, shortform_notes.md).

## Why viewers stop (the psychology)
- **Curiosity is a gap** in what someone knows (Loewenstein). The gap needs some prior knowledge, and it has to feel closable.
- **Curiosity peaks at about 50% certainty** (Kang & Loewenstein fMRI, 2009). If the viewer is sure of the answer, or has no idea, the hook is dead. Our audience already knows the famous stories, so the hook must add news to what they know.
- **The gap only pulls when the question is important, vivid and surprising** (Golman & Loewenstein). A trivia gap ("which word?") doesn't pull.
- **The strongest trigger is a broken expectation:** set up a pattern, then break it (Loewenstein).
- **Match the viewer's awareness** (Schwartz). For famous stories, lead with a new how or why, or with something about the viewer's identity, never the old claim.
- **Curiosity alone tests weakest** (Caples). Pair it with news, stakes or awe.
- **No blind lines** (Ogilvy). Each line must make sense alone and then lure the viewer on.
- **Concrete figures and images beat atmosphere** (Ogilvy; the Upworthy A/B tests).
- **Forward references** ("that same word") raise clicks. "This will teach you" and talking to the viewer lower them (Banerjee & Urminsky).
- **If the viewer can predict where it's going, they leave** (Halbert). The first line's only job is to get the second line read (Sugarman).

## Short-form rules (creators and platform data)
- **One clear subject and a reason to care, said fast.** If a line can be read two ways, viewers swipe (Kallaway).
- **Text, voice and visual say the same thing in the first second.** A mismatch is the most common failure (Kallaway).
- **Two lines, about 8 words each, not 28.** Break each line where you'd pause, and include a number or one strong word (Kallaway).
- **Lean in, then snap back:** state the familiar thing, then turn it (Kallaway).
- **It must read on mute:** at most 3 objects, centered, strong enough to be the thumbnail. Hint at the payoff and hold it for the end (Jenny Hoyos).
- **Treat the intro like a thumbnail:** familiar for the niche, plus one unexpected twist. Shorts with under 60% "viewed vs swiped" rarely do well (Paddy Galloway).
- **Show, don't announce,** and understood beats clever (the MrBeast memo; Brendan Kane, *Hook Point*).
- **Sends to non-followers matter most** (Mosseri). Aim for awe people forward, not a quiz.

## What fails (from the owner's rejections)
- **Bookkeeping before the mystery:** "eight lives end with the same word…" reads as counting, which is geeky.
- **Bare counts with no picture,** e.g. "seventh man".
- **Lines that end on an absence** ("…doesn't").
- **Story summaries and recaps of the purchase** (the "full price" hooks).
- **Talking about the audience,** question marks, or answering right away.
- **Clipped phrases the listener has to finish on first hearing.**
- **"Only", "never", "first" claims a commenter can disprove** (e.g. "only two people never died": Daniel, Ezra and others have no recorded death).

## Templates that worked
- **Famous subject plus one design claim:** "Every book of the Torah points to the middle" (110k+). "The Flood is a mirror" (100k+).
- **"Everyone in the Bible…", it's on purpose:** the well video, 264k in 4 days.
- **Exaggerated real number:** Veritasium's "96,000,000 black balls", 30M+.
- **Pattern, then break, with a concrete image:** "death gets the last word eight times / for one man, the last word is God."
- **Investigator:** a secret hidden in plain sight (the Quran pattern pages).

## Checklist before sending a hook
1. Would a stranger know the subject in 1 second, on mute?
2. Is there one vivid image, and does the visual show it in frame 1?
3. Does it break an expectation, or add news to something famous?
4. Is the viewer about half-sure of the answer, not certain and not clueless?
5. Is there awe of God or real stakes (death, love, Israel)?
6. Is it under about 10 words per line, with no question mark and no "you"?
7. Is every word true, with no "only", "never" or "first" a commenter can knock down?
