## Proven writing principles (researched Nov 2026)
- **MrBeast production memo (2024):**
  - The first minute matters most, and must match the title and thumbnail.
  - Re-engage at ~3:00 and ~6:00.
  - Stair-step: the stakes rise as the video goes on.
  - Strong payoffs; end abruptly; no dull moments; keep it simple.
- **Trey Parker and Matt Stone (NYU):** link beats with "but" and "therefore", never "and then".
- **Packaging:** the title and thumbnail set the promise, and the script has to honor it (Paddy Galloway, as reported).
- **BibleProject:**
  - "Say it once and say it simply."
  - Give full context, but assume an intelligent audience.
  - Anchor Hebrew words in their sound and an image.
  - Read drafts aloud, cut hard, and go through many drafts.
- **Veritasium (Derek Muller):** start from what viewers already believe, including misconceptions, then answer it.
- **Open loops:** 1–2 per video, and pay them off.

## Verified Hebrew, by topic
- **Goliath as the serpent:**
  - נְחֹשֶׁת is on Goliath four times (1 Sam 17:5–6). The Torah pairs it with the serpent: נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת (Num 21:9).
  - קַשְׂקַשִּׂים appears only in the fish laws (Lev 11; Deut 14), at 1 Sam 17:5, and for Pharaoh "the great dragon" (Ezek 29:3–4). תַּנִּין and נָחָשׁ are used for the same staff (Exod 7:10, 15).
  - מִצְחַת appears only at 17:6. The stone hits מִצְחוֹ twice in 17:49.
  - Gen 3:15, "he will strike your head": the "he" is the woman's offspring as a group, so don't frame it as one deliverer.
  - Saul is the tallest man in Israel (9:2) and afraid (17:11). His armor nearly repeats Goliath's (17:38, cf. 17:5).
  - הֵסִיר…מֵעַל three times: 17:26 (David's first words), 17:39, 17:46.
- **Nephilim:**
  - נְפִלִים appears only at Gen 6:4 and twice in Num 13:33. After the Flood, only the spies see them, and the Torah calls their report "evil" (13:32).
  - "Melted heart" appears in Deut 1:28 and in Rahab's words (Josh 2:11).
  - Anakim are left in Gath (Josh 11:22), and Goliath is from Gath (1 Sam 17:4).
- **Pharaoh's heart:**
  - In plagues 1–5, Pharaoh, or his heart, is the subject (7:13, 22; 8:11, 15, 28; 9:7). God first hardens it at the boils (9:12), then 10:20, 27; 11:10; 14:8.
  - The root כבד runs through: "heavy work" (5:9), the four "heavy" plagues, then "I will be glorified" (14:4, 17).
- **Esther:**
  - 167 verses, and no divine name anywhere.
  - Hidden names:
    - 1:20, first letters, backwards
    - 5:4, first letters, forwards
    - 5:13, last letters, backwards
    - 7:7, last letters, forwards
  - Base rate: about 1 per 2,444 words within a single verse; Esther has 3,045 words and 4 of them, about a 4% chance.
  - וּבַבִּזָּה לֹא שָׁלְחוּ אֶת יָדָם three times (9:10, 15, 16).
- **Hanukkah:**
  - The noun חֲנֻכָּה appears 8 times: Num 7:10, 11, 84, 88; Ps 30:1; Neh 12:27 (twice); 2 Chr 7:9.
  - Eighth days: Lev 9:1; 2 Chr 7:9; 2 Chr 29:17 (eight days, then eight more); Ezek 43:27. Ezra 6 counts no days.
- **Job:**
  - God repeats the narrator's words (1:1 → 1:8, 2:3).
  - חִנָּם: 1:9 and 2:3.
  - "Bless" meaning curse: 1:5, 1:11, 2:5, 2:9.
  - The wife echoes God (2:3 → 2:9).
  - "My servant Job" four times in 42:7–8.
- **Genesis 24 (short 119):** טֶרֶם and לְדַבֵּר appear together only in Gen 24:15 and 24:45; Isa 65:24.

## Newsletter evidence (Nov 5, 2026)
Full notes with sources are in the session scratchpad, `email_evidence.md`.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - MailerLite, religion (3.6M campaigns): 55.7% open, 2.95% click, 0.13% unsubscribe. MEASURED.
  - Apple accounts for 76.6% of tracked opens (Litmus, Sept 2026), so judge the newsletter by clicks, not opens.
- **Subject lines:**
  - Adding the first name lifted opens about 20% and cut unsubscribes 17% (1.2M-subscriber field experiments). MEASURED.
  - Keep the hook within about 40 characters (beehiiv).
  - In the Upworthy tests, headlines that were already concrete lost about 10% when made more concrete. Be specific, and leave one thing open.
- **Format:**
  - Plain or letter-style email beats designed HTML.
    - HubSpot: one image cost 21% of clicks.
    - NextAfter at First Baptist Dallas: clicks went from 0.8% to 1.7%.
  - One main link, placed first.
- **Day:** Tuesday to Thursday are best, and Thursday is never contradicted. Keep the same day and hour every week.
- **Opt-in:** Double opt-in subscribers start with about 2× the engagement (Mailchimp). Kit's free incentive email can do double opt-in, with the PDF behind the confirm button.
- **Kit free plan:**
  - Included:
    - up to 10k subscribers
    - broadcasts
    - tags and segments
    - forms and landing pages
    - the incentive email
    - A/B tests on 2 subject lines
    - Recommendations
  - Not included:
    - sequences
    - automations
    - RSS
    - polls
    - referrals
- **First 30 days:** Engagement peaks at the first email (Mailchimp). Make the incentive email the welcome email, and make every Thursday issue continue the "22 days" promise.
