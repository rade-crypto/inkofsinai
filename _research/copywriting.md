# Copywriting playbook: scripts, on-screen text, captions (researched Nov 3, 2026)

This goes with `hooks.md`. Read both before writing any script, reel text or caption. Full notes with sources are in the session scratchpad (copyresearch/classic_notes.md and shortform_notes.md).

## Body copy: keep them reading
- **Each line's job is to get the next line read.** End every beat with a seed of curiosity (Sugarman's slippery slide). Every line should reveal something new (Nicolas Cole, "rate of revelation").
- **Enter the conversation already in the viewer's head** (Collier). Start from the version they learned as kids, then turn it.
- **Copy can't create belief, only channel it** (Schwartz). Aim the viewer's existing reverence at one new detail. Build belief in small steps the viewer can nod to (Schwartz; Sugarman's chain of true statements).
- **Name the mechanism.** Show how the pattern works: the root, the count, where it sits. People who know "Bible codes" need something they can check, not a bigger claim (Schwartz).
- **Use specifics, not platitudes:** chapter, verse, word and count (Hopkins). Every claim carries its reason (reason-why).
- **Proof decides it. Claim less than you show** (Bencivenga). An honest limit ("this alone proves nothing") raises belief. Give the viewer something to check themselves (Heath, testable credentials).
- **One core idea.** "If you say three things, you don't say anything" (Heath).
- **Be concrete:** one person's moment, not "humanity" (Heath). Beware the curse of knowledge: every Hebrew word needs a sound or an image.
- **Link beats with "but" and "therefore", never "and then"** (Parker & Stone).
- **Plant every payoff early, and pay off everything you plant** (setup and payoff). Keep open loops short, and restate the question just before the payoff.
- **Write the way you talk.** Read it aloud the next day and edit (Ogilvy). Where you stumble, viewers leave (Halbert).
- **Vary sentence length.** "Don't just write words. Write music" (Provost).
- **Voice the skeptic in the third person** ("Some read it as…") and answer from the text (Kallaway; Veritasium).
- **Don't hedge verified facts.** "Maybe" gives viewers a reason to leave (Kallaway).
- **Name the pattern once, then reuse the name** (Kallaway, term branding).

## Structure and retention
- **Prove the hook with the first verse within 5–10 s** (Kallaway's lock-in).
- **Re-hook every 20–30 s in reels and every 60–90 s in long-form,** with a contrast word plus a new fact (Kallaway).
- **Pace it quick, medium, peak, medium, quick,** and let dense moments breathe (Hoyos).
- **Raise the stakes step by step:** letters, then people, then Israel, then God (MrBeast).
- **Never signal the end. Pay off, then cut** (MrBeast). Viewers remember the peak and the end, so the reveal goes last (Kahneman, peak-end).
- **Reel beats (50–58 s):**
  - 0–3 s: hook.
  - 3–8 s: lock-in verse.
  - 8–20 s: first instance (what they know, then "but", then the detail).
  - About 20 s: re-hook.
  - 20–35 s: escalation.
  - 35–45 s: the skeptic, answered.
  - 45–52 s: the reveal.
  - 52–58 s: the verse alone, then a hard cut.
- **Long-form beats (8 min):**
  - 0–1:00: promise plus the strongest proof. Open loop A and plant loop B.
  - 1:00–3:00: three instances.
  - About 3:00: re-hook.
  - 3:00–5:30: stakes rise, skeptic answered.
  - About 5:45: re-hook, pay off loop B.
  - 6:00–7:15: the deeper Hebrew.
  - 7:15–8:00: pay off loop A, verse, cut.

## On-screen text
- **Hook:** 2 lines of about 8 words each, held about 3 s.
- **After the hook,** the screen carries the nouns (the Hebrew word, the verse and reference, a 1–3 word label, the count) and the VO carries the argument. Reels without VO follow the script exactly.
- **At most one verse plus one label, or one diagram plus one line.** Hold a verse at about 15–17 characters a second, plus a beat.

## Why people share (Berger & Milkman; Berger, *Contagious*; Cialdini)
- **Awe raises sharing about 30%. Sadness lowers it about 16%.** Low-arousal emotion kills shares. Keep music and pace energetic, never meditative. Awe needs vastness plus a forced update to what the viewer knew (Keltner & Haidt).
- **Social currency:** give one sentence the viewer can repeat at the Shabbat table and look learned.
- **Practical value:** something to check ("open to Genesis 5").
- **Unity:** "our" Torah, this week's parsha. **Triggers:** post in the matching parsha week or holiday, and name it.
- **Rewatch:** the last line flows into frame 1. At the end, circle a detail that was visible from the start. Hold the finished diagram 2 s.
- **Save:** the finished chart as the reveal frame.

## Endings
- **The payoff answers the opening question and calls back the opening image.** The last thing on screen is the verse, alone.
- **No narration that restates the point,** and no "next time you read…".

## Captions
- **Line 1 (55 characters or fewer):** a second hook, with a concrete fact the reel doesn't say.
- **Then 2–4 lines with blank lines between:** the proof, verse references, and searchable names inside sentences. Search reads captions.
- **End:** the verse, then "Sources in the first comment."
- **At most 5 hashtags** (Instagram's cap).
- **One soft, specific send line:** "For whoever reads the parsha with you this Shabbat." Never "share if you agree" or "tag 3 friends".
- **Shorts:** keyword first in the title.

## AI-script failures to fix
- **Vague grandeur:** name the verse, word and count instead.
- **Robotic rhythm and empty triplets:** vary the length, and keep only items that carry a fact.
- **Stock contrast frames and rhetorical questions:** state the claim plainly.
- **"And then" lists and stacked proofs:** use but/therefore, one proof per beat.
- **Overclaiming and a moral at the end:** under-claim, check "only/every/first" in the WLC, and end on the verse.
- **Pictograph claims:** never say Hebrew letters carry meaning from ancient picture shapes. That idea is debunked.
