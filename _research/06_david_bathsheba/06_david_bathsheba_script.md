# 06 · David and Bathsheba: The One Word Behind the Crime. Voiceover, draft 10

**Title:** David and Bathsheba: The One Word Behind the Crime
**Thumbnail:** David on the palace roof at night, in gold ink. Glowing circle on the sealed letter in his hand. Text: "HE **SENT.**"

---
A soldier walks back to the front carrying a letter from the king, and the letter is his own death order.

King David wrote it. He has already taken this man's wife, and he commits both crimes from his own palace.

His orders go out through one Hebrew word: שָׁלַח. Send. In 2 Samuel 11, that word appears twelve times, and no chapter in the Bible has more.

But the next chapter is stranger. When God answers David, the answer is built out of the words of chapter 11, the words of David's crime, and one by one, they come back to him.

To see what comes back, first watch what goes out, starting with the first verse. Spring is "the time when kings go out" to war, but this king stays home and sends Joab, his general, with the army. And the spelling plays along: the word read aloud as "kings" is written הַמַּלְאֿכִים, with the letters of "messengers." In this chapter, a king's work is done by messengers, and soon David gives them a job no king should give.

From his roof, David sees a woman bathing, and he sends to ask who she is. The answer names her husband, Uriah the Hittite, one of David's own soldiers. David sends again: וַיִּשְׁלַח דָּוִד מַלְאָכִים וַיִּקָּחֶהָ, "David sent messengers, and took her."

When she sends word that she's pregnant, David sends for her husband and tells him to go home for the night, so the baby will pass as his. But Uriah sleeps at the palace door. His fellow soldiers are camped in the open field, he says, "and I should go home לֶאֱכֹל וְלִשְׁתּוֹת וְלִשְׁכַּב עִם אִשְׁתִּי, to eat, and to drink, and to lie with my wife?"

Eat, drink, lie with my wife. A chapter later, someone will say those three verbs to David's face, in the same order.

So the cover-up turns into a murder plan, and Uriah delivers it himself. David writes a letter to Joab, וַיִּשְׁלַח בְּיַד אוּרִיָּה, and sends it by the hand of Uriah: put him where the fighting is fiercest, then pull back, so he dies.

When the news comes back that Uriah is dead, David sends Joab a shrug: אַל יֵרַע בְּעֵינֶיךָ אֶת הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, "Don't let this thing be evil in your eyes. The sword devours this one and that one."

Count the sends, and the design shows. Eight of the twelve are David's, and Uriah never sends anything; he is only sent. The king never goes to her house, never goes near the battle, and never holds a sword. His hands are clean in every eye but one.

The chapter's last line takes David's shrug and turns it around: "The thing David had done was evil in the eyes of God." And after twelve sends, the thirteenth has a new sender: "And God sent Nathan to David."

Nathan brings the king a case to judge. A rich man has huge flocks, and a poor man has nothing but one little ewe lamb, raised with his children. מִפִּתּוֹ תֹאכַל וּמִכֹּסוֹ תִשְׁתֶּה וּבְחֵיקוֹ תִשְׁכָּב, "It ate from his bread, drank from his cup, and lay in his arms."

Eat, drink, lie: Uriah's three verbs, in the same order. Uriah gave up his home and his wife out of loyalty to David's army. In Nathan's story, that home comes back as a little lamb that eats at a poor man's table and sleeps in his arms.

But the lamb doesn't stay in his arms. A guest arrives, and the rich man won't take from his own flock. וַיִּקַּח, he takes the poor man's lamb, the same verb as "David sent messengers, and took her."

David has no idea the story is about him. He explodes: "The man who did this is בֶן מָוֶת, a son of death." And Nathan answers with two words: אַתָּה הָאִישׁ. "You are the man."

One echo could be chance. But the verdict keeps using chapter 11's words. "You struck Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and his wife you took for yourself." David had said the sword devours this one and that one. Now: לֹא תָסוּר חֶרֶב מִבֵּיתְךָ, "the sword will never leave your house."

And Nathan names what all those messengers kept out of sight: כִּי אַתָּה עָשִׂיתָ בַסָּתֶר, "You did it in secret." God will answer in the open, before all Israel and before the sun.

All chapter long, David sent others to do it. This time there's no messenger and no letter. David answers Nathan to his face: חָטָאתִי. "I have sinned against God."

And Nathan replies at once: "God has put away your sin. You will not die."

David sent to take and sent to hide, but God sent Nathan to bring him back. Years before, on the run for his life, David had prayed with the same verb: יִשְׁלַח מִשָּׁמַיִם וְיוֹשִׁיעֵנִי. "He will send from heaven and save me. God will send His love and His truth."

## Checked in the Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex)
- **Send:**
  - שָׁלַח appears 12× in 2 Sam 11: 11:1, 3, 4, 5, 6 (×3), 12, 14, 18, 22, 27.
  - No chapter in the Tanakh has more. Exodus 9 ties it at 12. In Samuel, the next highest chapters have 8.
  - Eight are David's: 11:1, 3, 4, 6 (to Joab), 6 ("send me", his order), 12 (אֲשַׁלְּחֶךָּ), 14, 27.
  - Joab has three (11:6, 18, 22). Bathsheba has one (11:5). Uriah is never the subject.
- **11:1:**
  - The Codex writes הַמַּלְאֿכִים with a silent א, tagged as "kings". BHS keeps Leningrad's form.
  - 1 Chr 20:1 has הַמְּלָכִים.
- **The roof:** 11:2 has גַּג בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ … מֵעַל הַגָּג. It is the only roof in the verse.
- **Bathsheba:**
  - Her only quoted words in 2 Samuel: הָרָה אָנֹכִי (11:5).
  - In 12:24 the read text says וַתִּקְרָא, "she named", but no words are quoted. She speaks in 1 Kgs 1–2.
- **Uriah:**
  - לֶאֱכֹל וְלִשְׁתּוֹת וְלִשְׁכַּב עִם אִשְׁתִּי (11:11), in booths, with the oath חַיֶּךָ וְחֵי נַפְשֶׁךָ.
  - 11:13 has וַיְשַׁכְּרֵהוּ, then "in the evening to lie … with his master's servants".
- **The letter:** 11:14–15. Joab places Uriah where the strong men are (11:16–17); the "pull back" step is never narrated.
- **The report:**
  - Joab's line about Uriah: 11:21.
  - כָּל אֲשֶׁר שְׁלָחוֹ יוֹאָב: 11:22.
  - אַל יֵרַע בְּעֵינֶיךָ אֶת הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה … כָזֹה וְכָזֶה תֹּאכַל הֶחָרֶב: 11:25.
- **The verdict:**
  - וַיֵּרַע הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה דָוִד (11:27); the on-screen Hebrew stops before the Name.
  - 12:1 follows 11:27, with God as the subject of שָׁלַח. Show it in English only.
- **The parable:**
  - 12:3 has מִפִּתּוֹ תֹאכַל וּמִכֹּסוֹ תִשְׁתֶּה וּבְחֵיקוֹ תִשְׁכָּב, the same roots in the same order as 11:11.
  - Taking the lamb (12:4); בֶן מָוֶת (12:5; the on-screen fragment leaves out חַי יְהוָה); אַרְבַּעְתָּיִם (12:6); אַתָּה הָאִישׁ (12:7).
- **The sword:**
  - 12:9 (English only) and לֹא תָסוּר חֶרֶב מִבֵּיתְךָ (12:10).
  - David's חָטָאתִי (12:13): show only חָטָאתִי on screen.
- **"Once more", not "last":** David sends again in 13:7.
- **Ending:**
  - 12:13 (English only); 12:14, 18 (the seventh day); 12:24, "God loved him" (English only).
  - וַיִּשְׁלַח בְּיַד appears in 2 Samuel only at 11:14 and 12:25; the only other place in the Tanakh is 1 Sam 16:20. The subject in 12:25 follows וַיהוָה אֲהֵבוֹ.
  - "Another son", because 1 Chr 3:5 lists four sons of Bathsheba.
  - יְדִידְיָהּ appears in English only; on screen show just יְדִיד.
- **Closing verse:**
  - Ps 57:1 is headed לְדָוִד.
  - Ps 57:4: יִשְׁלַח מִשָּׁמַיִם וְיוֹשִׁיעֵנִי … יִשְׁלַח אֱלֹהִים חַסְדּוֹ וַאֲמִתּוֹ. On screen, show the Hebrew only up to וְיוֹשִׁיעֵנִי and put "God will send…" in English.

- **Draft 9 echoes (chapter 11 → chapter 12):**
  - לקח: 11:4 → 12:4 (×2), 12:9, 12:10 (and 12:11).
  - "Evil … in eyes": 11:25 → 11:27 (and 12:9).
  - חֶרֶב: 11:25 → 12:9 (×2), 12:10.
  - Eat/drink/lie: 11:11 → 12:3.
  - בַסָּתֶר: 12:12, set against נֶגֶד הַשָּׁמֶשׁ. In 2 Samuel, it appears only at 12:12.
  - 12:1 is the 13th שָׁלַח, after the 12 in chapter 11.
