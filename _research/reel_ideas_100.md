# 100 reel ideas (Nov 20, 2026)

Every Hebrew claim below was checked against the Westminster Leningrad Codex (OSHB text). None is from the owner's 100-idea list or from a video in the notes. Divine Name verses go on screen in English.

**Top pick: #1, the Nephilim.** Highest search demand in the notes, one word to circle, and the claim can't be disproved.

## What the text doesn't say
1. The Nephilim are in two verses: נפלים (Gen 6:4; Num 13:33). After the Flood, the people who see them are the spies, and the Torah calls their report bad (Num 13:32).
2. Eden has no "sin" and no apple. The word חטאת first appears with Cain (Gen 4:7); the fruit is just פרי.
3. Adam was standing there: she gave "to her husband with her", עמה (Gen 3:6).
4. Genesis never uses the word שטן. In Job it is השטן, "the accuser", a title. In Num 22:22 the angel of God stands לשטן against Balaam.
5. Elijah didn't ride the chariot of fire. It separates the two men; he goes up בסערה, in the storm (2 Kgs 2:11).
6. Bathsheba wasn't on the roof. David was (2 Sam 11:2).
7. Delilah didn't cut Samson's hair. She called a man to do it (Judg 16:19).
8. Before Goliath, David is already called גבור חיל ואיש מלחמה (1 Sam 16:18).
9. The Torah never says "Ten Commandments". It says עשרת הדברים, "the ten words" (Exod 34:28; Deut 4:13; 10:4).
10. Noah took seven pairs of the clean animals: שבעה שבעה (Gen 7:2).
11. The tower of Babel is never destroyed. They "stopped building the city" (Gen 11:8).
12. Ezekiel names Sodom's guilt: pride, plenty of bread, and no hand for the poor (Ezek 16:49).
13. Jonah's fish is a דג גדול, and for one verse it turns feminine: דגה (Jonah 2:1, 2, 11).
14. "Let my people go" is half a sentence. Each time, it continues ויעבדני, "so they may serve me" (Exod 7:16, 26; 9:1, 13; 10:3).
15. The Passover animal could be a goat: מן הכבשים ומן העזים (Exod 12:5).
16. Job's wife says ברך, "bless God and die" (Job 2:9).
17. Isaiah says who the "fallen from heaven" poem is about: the king of Babylon (Isa 14:4, 12).
18. God tells Abraham והעלהו, "bring him up". The word "slaughter", לשחט, appears once in the chapter, in Abraham's hand (Gen 22:2, 10).
19. Noah says nothing through the whole Flood. His first recorded words are a curse (Gen 9:25).
20. The name Jerusalem never appears in the Torah.

## Designs inside one text
21. A mirror in six words: שפך דם האדם / באדם דמו ישפך (Gen 9:6).
22. Heart, ears, eyes, then eyes, ears, heart (Isa 6:10).
23. Jonah's sermon is five Hebrew words. The last, נהפכת, "overturned", is the verb used for a man turned into someone else (1 Sam 10:6). Nineveh was overturned.
24. Day two never gets "it was good". Day three gets it twice (Gen 1:10, 12).
25. Genesis 1 never says "sun" or "moon", only "the greater light" and "the lesser light" (Gen 1:16).
26. The seventh day has no "evening and morning" (Gen 2:1–3).
27. The Torah says יום אחד, "one day", then "second", "third" (Gen 1:5, 8).
28. Jeremiah sees creation run backwards: תהו ובהו returns, with no light, no human, no birds (Jer 4:23–25).
29. Psalm 29 says קול seven times.
30. Psalm 67 after its title: seven verses, 49 words, and the middle verse is the longest.
31. Song of Songs flips its own line: דודי לי ואני לו (2:16) becomes אני לדודי ודודי לי (6:3).
32. Two verses side by side give opposite advice about answering a fool (Prov 26:4–5).
33. Joel flips Isaiah: plowshares beaten into swords (Joel 4:10; Isa 2:4).
34. Naomi: "I went out full and came back ריקם" (Ruth 1:21). Boaz: "don't go back ריקם" (3:17).
35. Ruth quotes Boaz to Boaz: his "under whose wings", כנפיו (2:12), becomes her "spread your wing", כנפך (3:9).
36. Whose people? God to Moses: "your people, whom you brought up" (Exod 32:7). Moses to God: "your people, whom you brought out" (32:11).
37. The narrator says Aaron shaped the calf with a tool (Exod 32:4). Aaron says, "I threw it in the fire and out came this calf" (32:24).
38. The same sentence twice, two verses apart: fire comes out and consumes the offering, then Aaron's sons (Lev 9:24; 10:2). Aaron's answer is two words: וידם אהרן.
39. Saul's fall turns on the word קול: he didn't listen to the voice, "what is this voice of sheep?", "I listened to the voice of the people" (1 Sam 15:14, 19, 24).
40. Samson: "she is right in my eyes" (Judg 14:3). His eyes are gouged out (16:21). The book ends: "each did what was right in his eyes" (21:25).

## One Hebrew word
41. Hope is a rope. Rahab's cord is תקוה, the word for hope (Josh 2:18). Job makes the same pun at a loom (Job 7:6).
42. The rainbow is a weapon: קשת, "my bow", hung up in the cloud (Gen 9:13).
43. Abel's name, הבל, is the word Ecclesiastes repeats: vapor (Eccl 1:2). He never speaks in the text.
44. Naked and cunning are one verse apart and the same letters: ערומים, ערום (Gen 2:25; 3:1).
45. Babel in reverse: the builders say נלבנה, "let us make bricks". God says נבלה, "let us confuse" (Gen 11:3, 7).
46. Cain is sentenced to be נד, a wanderer. He settles in the land of נוד (Gen 4:12, 16).
47. Noah's name keeps sounding: the ark ותנח, the dove finds no מנוח, the smell is ניחח (Gen 8:4, 9, 21).
48. The manna is named "What?": מן הוא (Exod 16:15).
49. The verb behind Moses's name, משה, "draw out", appears in one other place: David's song, "he drew me out of many waters" (2 Sam 22:17; Ps 18:17).
50. Baby Moses is set בסוף, in the reeds (Exod 2:3). The nation is saved at ים סוף.
51. Moses is כבד פה, heavy of mouth (Exod 4:10). Pharaoh is כבד לב, heavy of heart (7:14).
52. Eli dies because he is כבד, heavy. The baby is named אי כבוד, "no glory". Same root (1 Sam 4:18, 21).
53. Samuel's birth story spells Saul's name: הוא שאול (1 Sam 1:28).
54. The Passover verb at Carmel: Israel is פסחים between two sides; Baal's prophets ויפסחו around the altar (1 Kgs 18:21, 26).
55. "I see an almond branch, שקד." "I am watching, שקד" (Jer 1:11–12).
56. A basket of summer fruit, קיץ. "The end, קץ, has come" (Amos 8:2).
57. One letter apart: he hoped for משפט and got משפח, for צדקה and got צעקה (Isa 5:7).
58. One letter decides who lives: שבלת or סבלת (Judg 12:6).
59. You can hear the horses: מדהרות דהרות (Judg 5:22).
60. Isaiah's שרפים are the same word as the burning serpents in the desert (Isa 6:2; Num 21:6).

## Two stories, one thread
61. Jonah and Elijah ask to die in the same words, וישאל את נפשו למות. The phrase appears in those two verses (1 Kgs 19:4; Jonah 4:8).
62. The golden calf happens twice. Jeroboam repeats Aaron's line, and his sons are נדב and אביה (Exod 32:4; 1 Kgs 12:28; 14:1, 20).
63. Esther reruns Saul: Mordecai is from Kish of Benjamin, Haman is an Agagite, and this time no one touches the plunder (Esth 2:5; 3:1; 9:10; 1 Sam 15:9).
64. Esther and Jacob speak the same sentence: כאשר אבדתי אבדתי, כאשר שכלתי שכלתי (Esth 4:16; Gen 43:14).
65. Joseph and Mordecai: the king takes off his ring, dresses him, and a herald calls before him (Gen 41:42–43; Esth 3:10; 6:11).
66. The Exodus in reverse: an Egyptian slave is afflicted, ותענה, by a Hebrew house and flees (Gen 16:1, 6; Exod 1:11–12).
67. Abraham lives the Exodus first: famine, down to Egypt, plagues on Pharaoh, sent away (Gen 12:10–20).
68. Boaz describes Ruth in the words of Abraham's call: she left father, mother and ארץ מולדתך (Ruth 2:11; Gen 12:1).
69. אשת חיל is in three verses. One names a woman: Ruth the Moabite (Prov 12:4; 31:10; Ruth 3:11).
70. The Torah bars a Moabite from the assembly (Deut 23:4). David's great-grandmother is one (Ruth 4:17).
71. "Why did you deceive me?", רמיתני, is said three times: Jacob to Laban, Saul to Michal, the medium to Saul (Gen 29:25; 1 Sam 19:17; 28:12).
72. Moriah appears twice: where Isaac was bound, and where Solomon built the Temple (Gen 22:2; 2 Chr 3:1).
73. Cherubim guard the way east of Eden (Gen 3:24). Cherubim are woven into the Tabernacle curtain (Exod 26:31).
74. The Tabernacle ends like creation: ויכל, the work, the seeing, the blessing (Exod 39:43; 40:33; Gen 1:31; 2:2–3).
75. Noah replays Eden: he plants, ויטע, takes the fruit, is naked, and a curse follows (Gen 2:8; 9:20–25).
76. After the Flood and after Sodom the same thing happens: wine, a father asleep, a family scandal (Gen 9:21; 19:33).
77. Miriam and water: she watches at the river, sings at the sea, and the verse after her death says "there was no water" (Exod 2:4; 15:20; Num 20:1–2).
78. A great man, איש גדול, listens to a נערה קטנה and comes out of the river with the skin of a נער קטן (2 Kgs 5:1, 2, 14).
79. Israel carried two arks through the desert. Joseph's coffin is an ארון (Gen 50:26; Exod 13:19).
80. Moses's mother and Rahab do the same verb: she hid him, ותצפנהו, ותצפנו (Exod 2:2; Josh 2:4).
81. Abimelech begs not to be remembered as killed by a woman. Joab remembers exactly that (Judg 9:54; 2 Sam 11:21).
82. Gideon says "I will not rule over you", then names his son אבימלך, "my father is king" (Judg 8:23, 31).
83. David sentences the rich man to pay fourfold, ארבעתים (2 Sam 12:6). Four of his sons die.
84. Why David's adviser turned on him: Bathsheba is the daughter of Eliam, and Eliam is the son of Ahithophel (2 Sam 11:3; 23:34).
85. The Torah gives a king three limits: horses, wives, gold. Solomon breaks all three within two chapters (Deut 17:16–17; 1 Kgs 10:14, 28; 11:3).
86. The Philistine god and the Philistine champion fall the same way: face down, head cut off (1 Sam 5:4; 17:49, 51).
87. Saul's spear is in his hand in scene after scene, and he dies leaning on it (1 Sam 18:10; 19:9; 22:6; 26:7; 2 Sam 1:6).
88. Samuel's robe, מעיל: his mother makes it, Saul tears it, and Saul knows the dead prophet by it (1 Sam 2:19; 15:27; 28:14).
89. Sarah says "my husband is old". God repeats it to Abraham as "I am old" (Gen 18:12–13).
90. After the binding, Abraham goes back to his servants; Isaac isn't mentioned. Next, Sarah dies in Hebron and Abraham "comes" there from Beersheba (Gen 22:19; 23:2).

## Spelling and scribal oddities
91. A cipher in Jeremiah: swap each letter with its opposite in the alphabet and ששך becomes בבל, and לב קמי becomes כשדים (Jer 25:26; 51:1, 41).
92. The Hebrew says Saul was "a year old" when he became king. The number is missing (1 Sam 13:1).
93. "When kings go out" is spelled המלאכים, "the messengers", in a chapter where David keeps sending them (2 Sam 11:1).
94. Ephron loses a letter in the verse where he takes the silver: עפרון, then עפרן (Gen 23:16).
95. Adam's "she gave me" is written הוא, the spelling of "he" (Gen 3:12).
96. Rebekah is written הנער, the spelling of "boy" (Gen 24:14).
97. One letter flips Job: written לא, "I have no hope"; read לו, "I hope in him" (Job 13:15).
98. The Levite clans add up to 22,300. The total given is 22,000 (Num 3:22, 28, 34, 39).
99. The upside-down נ comes back seven times in Psalm 107.
100. Later scribes wouldn't write "Baal" in a name: אשבעל becomes איש בשת, מריב בעל becomes מפיבשת, ירבעל becomes ירבשת (1 Chr 8:33–34; 2 Sam 2:8; 4:4; 11:21).

## Checked but not in the 100 (spares)
- First "love" in the Bible is a father's for his son (Gen 22:2); first "holy" is a day (Gen 2:3); first אמונה is Moses's steady hands (Exod 17:12); first "Torah" is before Sinai (Gen 26:5); first מזוזה has blood on it (Exod 12:7).
- No angel has a name until Daniel (Dan 8:16; 10:13).
- כשר is in three verses, none about food (Esth 8:5; Eccl 10:10; 11:6). פרדס is in three verses, none about Eden. גלמי appears once (Ps 139:16). לילית appears once (Isa 34:14). Tel Aviv is in Ezek 3:15. Cyrus is called משיחו (Isa 45:1).
- The Tanakh never calls its language "Hebrew"; it says יהודית (2 Kgs 18:26).
- David is דוד in Samuel and דויד in Chronicles. Joshua is ישוע once (Neh 8:17). וייצר has two י for the human, one for the animals (Gen 2:7, 19).
- Methuselah's years end in the year of the Flood (Gen 5:25–28; 7:6). The Torah's last letter and first letter spell לב.
- Dropped: "longest word" (three words tie at 11 letters); Ruth 3:5 "read but not written" (not marked in the OSHB text, so unverified).
