# Ink of Sinai scripts playbook

Read this in full before writing any script, Reel or Short text, hook, title or caption. Then run the checklist at the end.

It was built on Nov 5, 2026 from four sources:
- all 2,154 of the owner's messages (Oct 20 to Nov 5);
- every final script compared with its drafts (long-forms 01–06, the Ehud and Samson Reels, and 120+ Shorts);
- the hook and copywriting research (classic copywriters, curiosity studies, short-form creators, storytelling);
- the channel's view data.

`hooks.md` and `copywriting.md` are the short versions. `notes.md` holds the demand data and the verified Hebrew.

Quotes from the owner are in quotation marks, with dates as MM-DD.

## 1. The job

- **The goal is the best possible video: views, retention and accuracy.**
  - "facts dont mean views" (11-04).
  - Methods such as "clear pattern" or "hooks up front" are "a way to achieve it", not the goal (11-04).
  - "i dont want to correct you anymore, make it perfect" (11-04).
- **What the channel is:** a pattern-recognition Torah research channel that shows God's design in the Tanakh.
  - "not a moral stories profile but pattern recognizing Torah research profile" (10-24).
  - "focused on God how He designed it"; "Where is God in the script?" (10-28).
- **Our two differentiators:**
  - insight the viewer hasn't heard;
  - motion graphics that explain it.
- **Jewish, never Christian; Tanakh only.**
  - "We are not Christian page" (11-03).
  - Nothing should sound Christian up front. The Messiah tease was cut from 06 for this reason.
- **Evergreen, positive, never dark.**
  - Never tie Jews to trickery or end on guilt: "dont conclude with jews being wrong"; "i dont want to end on people thinking about their mistakes".
- **Use judgment.** Don't take instructions narrowly or literally, and push back when something won't serve the goal (11-04).
  - When asked to improve something, keep the angle that worked: "I meant just make it even better" (10-26).

## 2. Topic and insight

- **One real, checkable pattern with a deeper message.**
  - "you just narrated the story, not insightful, no differentiating factor" (11-03).
  - "the pattern is good but its missing second part" (11-01). Every pattern needs its "so what": what it means about God.
- **It must be proven and impressive.**
  - Model topics on the hits: "patterns words letters numbers etc as prof of Divine authorship" (10-26).
  - Use proven viral patterns found online and known patterns, not invented ones: "is that from the book or are you coming up with stuff"; "has anyone else noticed that before?" (11-04).
- **It must be verifiably real.** "Check which one is legit/true pattern" (11-01). The claim should win an argument: "something that someone might argue against but lose" (10-20).
- **No arguments from silence.** "So not mentioning something is somehow next best insight. Do better" (10-20).
- **The most amazing idea, not the easiest one:** "best idea not potentially best" (10-28). Be bold, and don't pull back on the risky idea (10-21).
- **Emotional and shareable beats clever:** "interesting but… not really emotional/shareable" (10-22).
- **Fresh material only.**
  - No reusing the last video: "You just redid the previous video word for word"; "too much of it is the same as in the video we just posted".
  - Never mention the previous video.
- **One theme per video.** Cover every on-topic instance and nothing off-topic (10-28, 10-29).
- **Learn from sources like Alter, never clone them.** "analyze his insights, you dont have to run the scripts around them exactly… dont be like a horse with blinders" (11-04).
- **Topic picking:** reviewers find the topic themselves, without candidates handed to them (11-03). Parasha clips go out on Fridays only.

## 3. Hooks

### The owner's rules
- **Stop the scroll.**
  - "Would you stop scrolling for those hooks cause i wouldn't" (11-03).
  - "Imagine the person scrolling always thinking 'why do i care'" (10-22).
- **Never open on what viewers already know.**
  - "the flood is famous? really? thats your hook?" (10-22).
  - "many people note the story… tying it to Jerusalem right away" (11-05).
  - "Everyone knows that… Make the hook about how the number itself repeats" (10-28).
- **The hook is not a plot summary.** "That's what happens yes but that's not what a good hook should say" (10-24).
- **Use a number, a contrast or a mystery.** This is the owner's model: "there are (number) barren woman in the Bible, they are sad to death but the last one is singing… that's how you write" (11-05).
- **Show several instances, not one.** "You can't just have one '40' in the hook" (10-28).
- **The 2 best lines, not 4** (11-01).
- **Keep the loop open.** "you just answered right away, that's not how hooks work" (10-31). Don't reveal right away (10-21).
- **No question mark, no "trick" wording, no contradiction** (10-23, 10-24).
- **Not geeky, scholarly or vague.**
  - "assuming the audience is Bible scholars" (10-24).
  - "counts its days is vague and weird" (10-22).
  - Don't build a hook on Hebrew: "Most people dont even speak hebrew" (11-01).
- **Not on someone viewers don't know or care about.** "most people dont know hannah nor care that she never met an angel… be clear on the pattern from the start" (11-04).
- **Don't open on death,** and don't open on the viewer's life: "shouldnt catch attention by mentioning viewer siblings" (10-31).
- **Never talk about the audience** (10-22, 10-24, 11-04: "i told you fifty times").
- **Clear on a scroll and fast.** The first 15 seconds can't be slow (10-28).

### The research
- **Curiosity peaks at about 50% certainty** (Kang & Loewenstein). The gap pulls only if it is important, vivid and surprising (Golman & Loewenstein).
  - So add news to a famous story; don't quiz on trivia.
- **Make a real claim and leave the gap in the how** (Scacco & Muddiman; Caples: curiosity alone tests weakest).
- **Lead with a new mechanism, not a bigger claim** (Schwartz, on audiences who have heard every claim). Show a root, a placement or a count they can see.
- **Lean in, then snap back** (Kallaway): the familiar picture, then turn it with one detail.
- **Enter the conversation already in the viewer's head** (Collier).
- **Treat the intro like a thumbnail** (Galloway; the MrBeast memo). Show the promise instead of announcing it. Text, voice and visual say the same thing in frame 1.
- **Wrap the obscure in a famous name,** and say that name within about 7 s (Kallaway's "cult hopping").
- **Use stakes words** (death, curse, blood) and cut praise adjectives. Negative words raised clicks; positive emotion words lowered them (Robertson; Banerjee & Urminsky).
- **No empty slots** ("this man", "the word") unless the thing is on screen; unfilled ones read as clickbait (Scott).
- **The payoff must beat the tease** (Loewenstein). Let the obvious guess form, then break it (Kang).

### Hooks that worked
- "Each book of the Torah / spells a word at its start." This led to "Every book of the Torah points to the middle" (110k+, 358 shares).
- "The Flood story / was written as a mirror. / At its exact center: / one verse about God." (100k+)
- "The Bible tells / the same love story / four times. / The last time, / the groom is God." (the well Reel; the long-form got 264k in 4 days)
- "40 keeps coming back / in the Bible. / It always means one thing." (20k in 2 days)
- "God saw that it was good. / 7 times. / Then He called one thing / 'not good.'"
- "In the book of Judges, / a king is stabbed so deep, / the fat closes over the blade." (Ehud, with VO)
- "Before Goliath says a single word, the Bible spends three verses on what he's wearing." (05)
- "Three times in Genesis, someone gets fooled, and each time the same three things show up…" (04)

**What they share:**
- a subject a stranger knows in one second;
- a claim that the text was designed;
- a turn that points at God or at a break, without saying how.

### Hooks that failed
- Bare counts: "Count every time God's name appears in the Torah" (5k).
- Breaks viewers already know: "Except every third one" (4.9k).
- "Same goat. Same garment. Same words." The ep20 list failed.
- Roll calls of names. The 06 draft 29 opened "Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah…".
- Retelling a famous line first, such as Rachel's "Give me sons" as the opener in the rejected Short drafts.
- Openings on someone obscure: the כָּעֵת חַיָּה opener got "i have no idea what youre saying".

## 4. Structure: story, never a list

- **Never a list.** "You're just listing them out, that's your script?" (11-05).
  - The test: if the beats could be shuffled without loss, it's a list.
  - Use one main character or object and one question running through, and make every beat change something.
- **Link beats with "but" and "therefore", never "and then"** (Parker & Stone).
  - Inside a beat, use "so", "until" or "which means". Overusing "but" sounds like friction (Kallaway).
  - "Years later…", "Then it's Judah's turn", "And it doesn't stop there" and "Remember this pattern" are all "and then" in disguise (ep118).
- **Every line makes the next one necessary** (Sugarman). End each beat on a pull line built from a fact.
  - "That razor line comes back once more, and this time no angel says it."
  - "Hannah gives her son away. The next mother has hers taken."
  - "Rebekah at least gets an answer. Jacob's wife gets nothing."
- **Every beat changes what the viewer believes** (McKee). If it doesn't, cut it.
- **One idea per beat and one proof per beat.**
  - "Too complicated, you lost me" (11-03).
  - "How is this relevant to the video's point?" (11-03).
  - Cut side proofs, even true ones.
- **Stakes climb:** letters, then people, then Israel, then God. The rungs go normal, then broken, then reversed, then national or divine.
- **Plant every payoff early and pay off everything you plant.**
  - Use one or two loops, and restate the question just before the payoff.
  - Don't explain an echo the viewer can already hear. 05 cut "It was the giant's own threat, turned around."
- **Re-hook with a contrast word plus a new fact:** every 20–30 s in Shorts, and every 60–90 s in long-form (around 3:00 and 6:00). Example: "And this is where the pattern breaks."
- **Slow down before the reveal** (Dicks's hourglass), and pause the VO after it.
- **Give the moral context the viewer needs.** "was this eglon guy bad, you didnt talk about that" (11-04). Finish every example: "no mention of his bride" (10-29).
- **Finish the story in the video, never in the caption** (11-04).
- **Never cut the best part.** "that was the actual good part" (10-22).
- **Before drafting, fill in this sentence:** "Nothing in ___ makes sense except in the light of ___" (Olson).

### Long-form formula (from the finals 01–06)
1. **Open** on one concrete scene or oddity, with the famous name in line 1.
2. **By about 0:30,** state the pattern in one sentence and plant two loops: one for mid-video, and one for the last instance, which is Israel or God.
3. **Pay off the title by about 0:45, then widen it** into a bigger question (Johnny Harris).
4. **Climb through the instances.** In each beat, tell the story in the present tense, give one Hebrew word in Hebrew letters with a quoted gloss and a scoped count, add one stance line, and end on a line that names what's next.
5. **End** by paying off the opening loop with a callback and the verse. Close on God's greatness, with no outro talk.

**Openings of the finals:**
- 03: "Abraham's servant travels to a far country, to find a wife for Isaac. He stops at a well. A girl comes out to draw water. Her name is Rebekah." The script shows it before naming it, then: "Three different men. Three different wells. The same scene."
- 05: "Before Goliath says a single word, the Bible spends three verses on what he's wearing. Almost all of it is bronze. In Hebrew, bronze is נְחֹשֶׁת. The serpent in Eden is נָחָשׁ."

### Shorts formula (from the hits)
1. **The hook:** two short lines in a scene of their own. Name the famous subject, claim it was designed, and add a turn that points at God or the break without saying how.
2. **The lock-in** by about 3–8 s: the first verse or count on screen proves the hook.
3. **3–8 instances,** each changing one thing so the run climbs. Each instance is a verse banner with the key word lit in Hebrew and English.
4. **A re-hook at 20–35 s,** a fair check for the skeptic, and the break landing on God at 45–60 s.
5. **The close:** a two-line callback to the hook, then the verse alone, then cut. A CTA card goes only on cut-downs of long-forms.

**Length:**
- 55–75 s.
- Text-only Shorts: 60–150 words on screen.
- With VO: about 140–160 words, at 2.3–2.9 words a second.

**Models:**
- **The Flood:** a visible mirror shape, then the one slot without a number holds God.
- **Samson:** one changed sentence and both versions come true.
- **Ehud:** one scene, where each Hebrew word is the next step of one argument, ending "The offering was the king."

## 5. Voice and sentences

- **Human rhythm.**
  - "writing like ai and all sentences being same length" (11-04).
  - "chop up your sentences to sound dramatic" (11-01, a complaint).
  - Mix sentences of 2 to 25 words, averaging about 14 (the 05 and 06 finals).
  - No more than two sentences under 6 words in a row. Save fragments for reciting the pattern ("No child. A long wait.") and for the reveal ("Begin.").
  - Use cumulative sentences that add detail like a camera move (Christensen). Write in paragraphs, never one line per sentence.
- **The narrator has a stance.**
  - Action, then one line of meaning (Ira Glass).
  - Dry asides: "Laban's answer stings if you remember what Jacob did."
  - Lines that sound like us are symmetrical reversals with the strongest word last:
    - "Rachel asked for sons or death, and she got both."
    - "The only sword in the fight was Goliath's, and it took his head."
    - "Isaac failed to recognize what was right in front of him, and Jacob recognizes something that isn't true."
    - "Israel brought the king an offering. The offering was the king."
- **Clear on first listen, and literally sensible.**
  - "They didnt build a tower with a word, youre not making sense" (10-24).
  - "It sounds like saul is the boy" (11-03).
  - "what do you mean start out?" (11-04).
  - No ambiguity, no vague words, no repeated words or names ("You say tall to much").
- **Quote enough to make sense** ("to the day of his death what?"). Explain names and idioms ("'at the time of life'… means next year").
- **Concise, but explain fully.** "you talk too much" and "too many words", but also "dont worry about length… explain fully". Every word must earn its place; no filler words "just to fill up space".
- **Modern English.** "change the hast and those old words". Use English names in the VO (Jacob, not Yaakov).
- **Banned AI phrasing:**
  - "It's not X, it's Y"; "X isn't about A, it's about B"; "not just X, but Y";
  - "Here's the thing"; "This changes everything"; "Let that sink in";
  - rhetorical questions answered right away;
  - empty triplets and "clause trios";
  - closing lines that restate the point;
  - "-ing" tails that claim significance;
  - instructions pasted in as lines: "'That's how great God is' really? That's your copywriting?" (11-03).
- **Never describe the audience:** no "most people", "most of us", "we were taught" or "most readers stop here". Plain phrasing such as "gives us" is fine; don't overcorrect.
- **Voice the skeptic as a statement about the text, then answer it from the text.** For example: "One shared word could be chance. Three, in the same order, is harder to explain."
- **No hedges on verified facts.** Cut "maybe" and "perhaps"; verify or drop. Don't undermine yourself: "'the other three bend it' why would you put that there".

## 6. Hebrew in scripts

- **Always in Hebrew letters, never transliterated.** "send the script with hebrew words not pronounciation"; "always spell Hebrew words in Hebrew".
- **If the script mentions a Hebrew word, the VO says it:** "you say hebrew word for forehead etc but you dont say it". Give the Hebrew, then the English gloss, because "the voiceover will say both".
- **The Hebrew comes before an English verse quote** (11-04).
- **Anchor every word in its sound, an image or an echo:**
  - "Say them together, and you can hear the serpent inside the bronze."
  - "A 'forehead' on his legs."
- **Use scoped counts**, such as "In Hebrew, those exact words appear in just two verses". Say "same root" or "same letters", never invent an etymology.
- **One spine word per video,** flagged each time it returns: "And there's our word again."
- **Pronunciation matters** ("it shows we don't pronounce Hebrew right"). Mark the stress where the voice may slip (רָנִּי) and check names ("sah-ruh instead of serah").

## 7. Accuracy

- **Only what the text says.** "dont add details you arent sure are in the text"; "coat of many colors where does it say that"; "Was jacob really blind"; "David told the giant this would happen… Where?"
- **Verify every count, reference and root in the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC).** "only", "every", "first", "last" and "exactly" must be checked or dropped. Qualify "first" by canonical order.
  - The owner once said "use the scrolls, not the Leningrad codex" (10-26), but CLAUDE.md is later and stands: the WLC.
- **Keep interpretation separate from the text,** and translate conservatively ("struggle", not "crush"). Don't stretch a pattern past the text (Rebekah has no rival wife).
- **Watch for the objections a commenter will raise.** "Would someone say Solomon isn't a prophet?"
- **Tanakh only as authority.** Say "God", never "Hashem", and no Divine Name in on-screen Hebrew. Keep manuscripts off screen ("Dont mention leningrad codex"). Outside sources go in the pinned comment.
- **Name people where the Bible names them.** Unnamed is fine: "if theyre not named its fine, obviously".
- **Facts are the floor.** After the fact-check, rework every line for attention (11-04).

## 8. Endings and takeaways

- **End on the point, as an emotional, general message that praises God, then a verse.**
  - "message on how great God is and end with a verse like always".
  - "Where is the emotional general message at the end etc like in all our videos?" (11-03).
  - "a line at the end with the point of the video, like a message that praises God" (11-05).
- **The takeaway is the viewer's point, not more analysis or a truism.**
  - "we know God does everything"; "its still analyzing the story, lets get the takeaway" (11-05).
  - The 06 final line: "It came from God."
- **Do the last posted video's closing job without copying its words:** "you made the last line too similar, use your brain a bit".
- **End on the wonder, not the last event:** "not just end at God buried him but conclude how amazing that is" (10-26).
- **Balance emotion and insight:** "too much on the emotional side, it should be a balance" (10-24).
- **The final verse ties up the message.** It should be strong, touching and awe-inspiring for a beginner ("leviticus 3:16 is too serious").
- **Make it a message for everyone.** Not praise of Jews, not guilt, not a self-help moral. The hits never end on "Maybe that's the invitation…".
- **The story needs a real ending:** "looks like it ends halfway through".
- **Never signal the end.** Pay off, verse, cut (MrBeast; peak-end). For Shorts, let the last line flow back into frame 1.
- **For a Short cut from a long-form:** keep it coherent, insightful and inspiring, with a takeaway, then a "Full video on YouTube" card showing the thumbnail and title, "like you did for the well one".

## 9. Voiceover delivery

- **The owner records the VO.** Send only the lines to record, as plain text with the Hebrew in Hebrew letters. After any fix, send the whole script again.
- **One voice and one accent.** No British accent, not too loud, no pitch-shifting. Key words must be audible, with a short pause after each reveal ("'taken' is said too quietly… tiny pause").
- **Patches must match the original recording** ("'Where' sounds like its from another audio").

## 10. Titles, thumbnails, captions, social

- **Titles:**
  - The searchable keyword plus the insight, with nothing a commenter can disprove.
  - Write them as a scroller would read them, from real research ("You obviously did zero research").
  - Not too dark, not too long, and clearly about the Bible. Don't center them on obscure people.
  - Winners: "Why Everyone in the Bible…", "The Deception the Bible Keeps Repeating", "Goliath Was Dressed as the Serpent", "Why So Many Women in the Bible Were Barren".
- **Thumbnails:**
  - Gold on black, one subject, a 2–3 word statement ending with a period, and a glowing circle on the reveal only.
  - Don't repeat the title, and don't claim anything obvious ("everyone knows God is the groom").
  - Not boring, no reused art, no spiral, no text stroke.
  - Proven: "IT'S ON PURPOSE." For 06: "NOT A COINCIDENCE." / "not an accident".
- **Captions:**
  - Short lines with a blank line between them, no emojis, no recap of the video, no "stay tuned".
  - Line 1 is a second hook, built on a fact the video doesn't say.
  - Every line must make sense and be interesting.
- **YouTube description:** a normal description, with no verse quotes.
- **Comment replies:** a few words that still say why, argued as the author. Humble, not try-hard, no emojis.
- **Polls:** short, adult and personal, never a quiz, and never answered by the video ("The answer is the hook though").
- **Other:**
  - Tags come from autocomplete research.
  - Strip `?si=` from links.
  - The audio name is short and doesn't start with "Ink of Sinai".
  - IG story: little text, and room for the link.

## 11. On screen (the script must be written for the screen)

The full rules are in CLAUDE.md. These bear on the writing:
- **Text follows the VO exactly and appears on the word, never late.** The screen carries the nouns (the Hebrew word, the verse and reference, a 1–3 word label); the VO carries the argument.
- **Every quoted verse appears on screen:** reference, Hebrew with the key word lit, and English below. Show "just the necessary ones", and hold each one long enough to read.
- **Make the pattern visible.** Use a collage or diagram where the same scene happens twice, and give every number or label context ("7 7 40 150… without any other context isnt enough").
- **Cut labels the VO doesn't need,** and don't crowd the screen. No small text and no middle dots.
- **Big closing lines get their own scene.** End on the verse alone.

## 12. Working with the owner

- **Answer first, in one short message, then work.** Send only what was asked for, and change nothing else ("just remove fade out and nothing else").
- **Don't overcorrect.** "now its completely different, not what i meant".
- **No questions in go mode ("go, no questions").** Work fast, skip unneeded steps, and run the needed checks.
- **Watch and review everything as a first-time viewer before sending.** "What do you mean you haven't watched it. Watch it."
- **Reviewers:** three viewer reviewers on every script (a casual fan, a Hebrew-reading skeptic, a secular scroller), plus a fact and skeptic pass. Keep them quick, and match the number the owner asks for.
- **Never say you can't.** Make it in-house. Don't generate images or spend credits unless asked.
- **Remember what was agreed.** "did you forget everything we talked about"; "you see how many instructions i have to give you?" That is why this file exists.

- **Conflicts, resolved for this channel:**
  - **Strongest first vs reveal last:** prove the title with the best case in minute 1, and hold the "what it says about God" layer for the end.
  - **Curiosity vs substance:** state the claim and withhold the mechanism.
  - **Extreme vs exact:** put the extremity in the image, the verb and the stakes, and keep counts exact.
  - **Staccato hooks vs AI fragment chains:** short lines go only in the hook and the reveal. The narration averages 12–15 words a sentence and never needs two breaths.
  - **Negativity shares vs a reverent channel:** the stakes live in the story (death, deception, curse) and never point at viewers or other faiths. Awe is the target.

## 14. Before sending any script: the checklist

1. Can I fill in the sentence "Nothing in ___ makes sense except in the light of ___"? Is it a real pattern, with a deeper message about God, that viewers haven't heard?
2. Does the hook:
   - say something new about a famous subject, in at most 2 lines;
   - use a number, a contrast or a mystery;
   - keep the loop open;
   - avoid a question mark and "you";
   - avoid opening on the known story, a roll call of names, an obscure figure or Hebrew;
   - pass the hooks.md checklist?
3. Does the first verse or proof lock it in by about 6 s (Shorts) or 0:45 (long-form)?
4. Is it a story, not a list? Check that there is one character or object, one running question, beats joined by "but" or "therefore", every beat changing something, and nothing that could be shuffled.
5. Do the stakes climb toward Israel and God? Is there a re-hook at 20–35 s (Shorts) or around 3:00 and 6:00 (long-form)? Is every planted payoff paid off?
6. Does every line make sense on first listen and make the next line necessary? Cut the lines that sound like an ending before the end.
7. Is the rhythm varied (2–25 words, about 14 on average, no more than two fragments in a row)? Is there no banned phrasing, no talk about the audience, and no hedging?
8. Is the Hebrew in Hebrew letters, spoken in the VO with its gloss, anchored in a sound or an image, with every count scoped?
9. Is every claim in the text and checked in the WLC, with no "only/every/first/last" left unchecked, and nothing Christian-sounding?
