# Sell a Genesis pattern guide before Hanukkah

Ink of Sinai's first product should be a **$17 "Hidden Patterns in Genesis" PDF study guide, sold on Kit Commerce, with a comment-keyword DM funnel into a free lead magnet and a Kit welcome sequence, launched by mid-November and pushed hard over Hanukkah (Dec 4–12, 2026)**. The accounts most like Ink of Sinai that earn real money do it two ways. Torah and Hebrew pattern teachers (Aleph Beta, Chaim Bentorah) sell **$15–20/month memberships**. Women's Bible-study brands (She Opens Her Bible, Daily Grace Co.) sell **$5–35 PDFs and printables**. The only hard revenue figures come from nonprofit tax filings. Aleph Beta, the closest format match, reported **$3.23M revenue in FY2025, $2.40M of it earned from programs** (mostly subscriptions). That took more than a decade and a named teacher. No faceless creator at ~10k followers has public revenue data. Benchmark math says a first launch will earn **hundreds to low thousands of dollars**, not more, and the size of the email list sets the ceiling. So the job right now is to turn viral reach into Kit subscribers and give them one well-made, verifiable, low-priced product, timed to the Genesis portions and Hanukkah.

## Torah-pattern content already pays, through memberships

The strongest proof that "hidden structure in the Hebrew text" can earn money is **Aleph Beta**. It charges **$18/month or $15/month billed annually**, with a free trial and a money-back guarantee, and claims "12,000+ subscribers" and "10,000+ paying members" ([Aleph Beta pricing](https://www.alephbeta.org/pricing)). Its patron tiers run from **$999 to $5,000 a year** ([Patrons Circle](https://www.alephbeta.org/patrons-circle)).

The research notes disagreed on whether Aleph Beta's tax filing exists. The sources settle it: **the filing exists, under a different legal name.** One researcher searched ProPublica for "Aleph Beta" and found only similarly named organizations. The other found the 990 under EIN **27-3846145**, filed as "Hoffberger Fund For Biblical Studies Ltd". Aleph Beta's own donate page names its 501(c)(3) as the Hoffberger Institute for Text Study with the same EIN ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/273846145); [Aleph Beta donate](https://www.alephbeta.org/donate)). CauseIQ lists the same EIN under the Aleph Beta name ([CauseIQ](https://causeiq.com/organizations/aleph-beta,273846145)).

The filing reports **FY2025 revenue of $3,228,300**, made up of $2,398,701 program revenue and $906,683 contributions. Program revenue grew from **$7,942 in FY2013** to $2.40M in FY2025 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/273846145)). The filing also cross-checks the self-reported claims. Ten thousand payers at $180–216 a year would bring in about $1.8–2.2M, close to the reported $2.4M. Contributions are about 28% of revenue, which contradicts Aleph Beta's statement that it is funded entirely by subscribers ([Jewish Link](https://jewishlink.news/experience-tisha-b-av-with-aleph-beta/)).

Christian-facing Hebrew teachers follow the same model at smaller scale. **Chaim Bentorah** uses a free daily word study to sell a **$0.99 trial that becomes $19.99/month** ([hebrewwordstudy.com](https://www.hebrewwordstudy.com/)). **Jeff Benner's Patreon** shows how small this model is without a big audience: an estimated **134 paying patrons and $348–$1K a month** ([Graphtreon](https://graphtreon.com/creator/jeffbenner)). Messianic organizations bring in more money, but most of it is donations rather than product sales. Fusion Global, which teaches "the significance of Hebrew words, numbers, and holidays", got **85.8% of its $3.15M** FY2024 revenue from contributions ([philanthropy.org](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/475619402/fusion-global-inc)). First Fruits of Zion got about $3.62M of its ~$5.0M from contributions ([CauseIQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/first-fruits-of-zion,841233209)).

The pattern across these organizations is that Jewish buyers pay for structured study, while Christian Hebrew-curious buyers are the larger group and are used to buying books and giving. That conclusion rests on only four organizations. Ink of Sinai's neutral "patterns in the text" angle can sell to both groups if it stays out of theology.

## Women's Bible-study brands win with $9–27 PDFs

The closest match for a mostly female audience is **She Opens Her Bible**. It sells a **$16.98 Biblical Hebrew for Beginners course**, a **$27 starter bundle** and **$9 studies**, and uses a **free AI Hebrew word-study tool** as its lead magnet ([sheopensherbible.net](https://www.sheopensherbible.net/); [Hebrew Word Study Tool](https://www.worthbeyondrubies.com/hebrew-word-study/)).

The big brands show how the model scales. **Daily Grace Co.** started as **one Bible study on Etsy** and now claims "over 8 million books sold" (self-reported) ([Westminster Bookstore](https://wm.wts.edu/read/the-daily-grace-company)). Zach Windahl self-published *The Bible Study*, grew a following on TikTok and Instagram, and reports **500,000+ copies sold** (publisher-reported) ([Rush to Press](https://rushtopress.org/zach-windahls-the-bible-simplified-surpasses-100000-copies-sold-in-its-first-year-reflecting-a-new-generations-hunger-for-scripture/)). Well-Watered Women is now relying less on social media and more on "high-value emails and evergreen Bible study tools" ([wellwateredwomen.com](https://wellwateredwomen.com/next-season)).

Platform data points to the same price band. Third-party estimates say **over 50% of Stan Store sales are downloads priced $4–$30** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/stan)). The generic Etsy faith-printable market is crowded, though. One new shop reached **1,069 sales in its first year** ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/shop/HolyDesignCollective)), while many others sit at 1–12 sales ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/shop/DailyRootedStudio)). Ink of Sinai's counts and patterns are distinctive, so its product should sell to its own audience, not compete in Etsy search.

## Quran creators show a count-based hook can lead to education products

No product or store could be found for faceless Quran word-count pages such as "Frames of Quran" or "Quran Marvels". Most of the visible money in that niche comes from selling reel templates to other creators, for example a **~$10 pack of Quran reels** ([Gumroad](https://deenbound.gumroad.com/l/quranreels)). The businesses that earn real money teach the original-language text.

**Arabic Unlocked** (28,699 Instagram followers) uses count-based hooks like "16 phrases that appear over 450 times in the Quran" to lead viewers into an app and paid courses ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/arabicunlocked/)). That is almost exactly Ink of Sinai's format. Dina Aziz's **free Ramadan planner** reportedly reached a million downloads in 24 hours and led to a Penguin Random House book ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dina_Aziz)). Free seasonal tools can do more for growth than paid ones.

Etsy searches for "quran journal" rose from 5,400 to **9,900 a month just before Ramadan** ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/quran-journal)), so holiday products should launch 4–8 weeks early.

Credibility is a real risk. Muslim scholars have publicly criticized "numerical miracle" content as containing "discrepancies and some outright fabrications" ([IslamHouse](https://islamhouse.com/en/articles/190446/)). A paid Ink of Sinai product should show its counting method and sources so it cannot be dismissed as "Bible codes."

## Benchmarks point to an email-first, low-fee launch

Every revenue figure in this section is a benchmark or an estimate.

**Conversion rates.** A "good" rate from social traffic to email signup is **2–5%** of visitors ([beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/convert-email-subscribers-from-social-media)). Email-to-purchase rates in general e-commerce are **1–3%** ([Monday.com](https://monday.com/blog/monday-campaigns/email-conversion-rates/)). A passive bio link converts only about 0.1–0.5% of profile visitors, and that figure comes from a non-faith funnel ([Inrō](https://inro.social/blog/instagram-to-onlyfans-conversion-benchmarks)).

**Comment-to-DM automation.** Vendors and agencies claim comment-to-DM opt-in rates of **15–80%** ([SmartReply](https://smartreply.io/blog/instagram-comment-dm-automation-guide); [GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/dm-automation-instagram-collect-emails-manychat)). The best-known case, Jenna Kutcher's **$1.7M and 53k leads**, is vendor-reported and comes from a ~1M-follower account ([CaseStudies/Manychat](https://www.casestudies.com/company/manychat/case-study/jenna-kutcher-drives-17m-in-sales-with-manychat)). Treat all of these as directional.

**Estimated launch revenue.** These are estimates, not reported results. A list of **500–1,500 subscribers** buying a $17 guide at 1–5% would bring in roughly **$85–$1,300 per launch**. A list of about 5,000 would bring in roughly **$1k–$4k**.

**Platform fees.** **Kit Commerce** charges **3.5% + $0.30 with no monthly fee, even on the free plan**, which is about $0.90 on a $17 sale ([Kit Help Center](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/4199324-kit-commerce-overview-faqs)). Gumroad takes 10% + $0.50 ([Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/pricing)). Stan Store costs **$29/month** ([SchoolMaker](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/stan-store-pricing)) and only beats Kit's fee above roughly $830/month in sales. One caveat: Kit is not a merchant of record, so VAT on non-US sales is Ink of Sinai's responsibility.

## Recommendations, in priority order

**1. Build the lead magnet and DM funnel this week (late October).** Make a free 5–7 page PDF, for example "7 Hidden Patterns in the Joseph Story" or a Hebrew letter-and-count reference chart, delivered through a Kit form. Add a comment-keyword DM automation ("comment JOSEPH") to every reel that is performing well, plus a 4-email Kit welcome sequence that delivers the PDF, teaches one more pattern, then offers the paid guide. Today almost none of the reach that runs far beyond the follower count turns into email subscribers, and this step fixes that.

**2. Launch the first product by about November 16: "Hidden Patterns in Genesis," a PDF study guide at $17.** Use $12 as a launch price for the list for 72 hours. Sell it on Kit Commerce. Give each portion one or two verifiable patterns with the counting method shown, plus women-friendly study pages: a journaling prompt and a printable visual. The $17 price sits inside the band where most creator sales happen and next to She Opens Her Bible's $16.98 Hebrew course.

**3. Make Hanukkah the main sales push (Dec 4–12).** Shabbat Hanukkah 2026 falls on **Vayeshev (Dec 5) and Miketz (Dec 12)**, the Joseph portions. Offer a "Hanukkah gift edition" of the guide, with a gift option or a bundle that includes a free 8-night Hanukkah pattern card set. Run one reel a night with the keyword CTA. Hold an "end of Genesis" last call after Vayechi (Dec 26).

**4. Release the next book on January 2.** Release "Hidden Patterns in Exodus" timed to Shemot. Offer Genesis buyers the two as a $27 bundle, which tests whether people buy book by book.

**5. Wait on the membership.** Start a $9–15/month "Pattern of the Week" membership only after the list is several thousand strong and two guides have sold. Memberships are proven at the top of this niche, but a Patreon with ~134 paying members earns hundreds a month. Use Etsy as an optional mirror for discovery, not as the main shop.

## Conclusion

The comparables show that the market buys a fixed sequence of products, and the order matters. Proof of rigor and an owned audience come first, a low-priced owned product comes next, and a recurring membership comes only after enough trust and scale. Aleph Beta's 12-year rise from $8k to $2.4M in earned revenue shows how high the ceiling is for Torah-pattern content. Benner's Patreon shows how low the floor is without scale. Ink of Sinai's advantage over both is reach, and that advantage is wasted until reach becomes email subscribers.

Two things are still unknown and should be measured during the Hanukkah launch. The first is whether faceless delivery lowers willingness to pay; no data exists on this either way. The second is whether the audience leans Jewish or Christian, which decides whether the second product is a parasha-cycle membership or a Christian-framed feast or Hebrew-word guide. Tagging buyers in Kit by which lead magnet they came from will answer the second question for free.
