# Jewish, Torah, Hebrew-language and Hebrew-roots creators who sell digital products

Research date: 2026-10-26. Context: comparables for Ink of Sinai (@inkofsinai), a ~10k-follower faceless IG/TikTok/YT account on Torah letter patterns, gematria and stories. Note: live follower counts could not be pulled (Instagram/TikTok/Social Blade/Patreon pages were blocked or returned nothing usable to the fetch tool), so follower counts are mostly gaps. Revenue evidence is strongest for nonprofits via IRS Form 990 data.

## Who monetizes Torah/Hebrew content with digital products, and at what prices?

### Takeaway
The clearest proven model is a paid Torah-video membership. Aleph Beta (Rabbi David Fohrman) charges $15–18/month plus $999–$5,000/yr patron tiers, and in FY2025 it reported $3.23M revenue, $2.40M of it program-service (mostly subscription) revenue, per its Form 990. Christian-facing Hebrew-roots and Messianic brands (Fusion Global/Rabbi Jason Sobel, First Fruits of Zion, Chaim Bentorah, Hebrew4Christians, Jeff Benner) earn mainly from donations, books and memberships. Small creators sell $7–$25 printables, ebooks and Patreon tiers. I found no public revenue figures for any individual faceless creator at Ink of Sinai's size.

### Cited Findings

**1. Aleph Beta (Rabbi David Fohrman; Jewish, parasha/Torah video explainers). This is the closest format match: animated, pattern-focused Torah videos.**

- Self-reported scale: "1,200+ videos and podcasts", "12,000+ subscribers" and "10,000+ paying members". The page gives both figures without explaining the difference, so treat them as self-reported — [Aleph Beta pricing](https://www.alephbeta.org/pricing). The team page claims "180k users" (likely free and paid combined) — [Aleph Beta team](https://www.alephbeta.org/team)
- Patron tiers: Bronze $999/yr, Silver $1,800/yr, Gold $5,000/yr. Perks include live patron-only webinars with Rabbi Fohrman, in-person events, gift accounts and a semester-long Biblical Hebrew course — [Aleph Beta Patrons Circle](https://www.alephbeta.org/patrons-circle)
- 990 data (EIN 27-3846145, listed on ProPublica as "Hoffberger Fund For Biblical Studies Ltd". The donate page names the 501(c)(3) as "Hoffberger Institute for Text Study, Inc.", same EIN) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/273846145); [Aleph Beta donate](https://www.alephbeta.org/donate):
  - FY2025: revenue $3,228,300; program-service revenue $2,398,701; contributions $906,683; expenses $3,287,475
  - FY2024: revenue $3,185,287; program-service revenue $2,256,467
  - FY2021: revenue $2,815,273; program-service revenue $1,608,248
  - FY2015: revenue $796,159; program-service revenue $367,630
  - FY2013: revenue $479,238; program-service revenue $7,942
  - Program-service (earned) revenue grew roughly 300x from FY2013 to FY2025 and is now about 74% of revenue.
  - FY2025 officer pay: Immanuel Shalev (CEO) $165,263; David Fohrman $150,891 — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/273846145)

### Inferences
- The Aleph Beta trajectory (program revenue $8k in 2013 to $2.4M in 2025, at $15–18/mo) shows that people will subscribe to Torah videos about patterns and hidden structure. It took more than a decade, a named teacher and a nonprofit structure to get there.
- Faceless 10k accounts are unlikely to support a subscription at first. The evidence points to cheaper entry products: $7–25 printables or ebooks, a "40 Hebrew words" style ebook built from an existing video series, and Patreon tiers. Small Torah Patreons appear to have tens of members (for example, about 45).
- Every Christian-facing brand found (Bentorah, Hebrew4Christians, Benner, Sobel) runs the same funnel: free daily word, then email, then books or membership, then donations.

- Live follower counts for all accounts (IG, TikTok, YT) as of 2026 could not be retrieved. Social Blade and the platforms were not reachable through the tools.
- I found no Graphtreon or Patreon earnings data (Patreon returned 403 and Graphtreon search returned 404).
- I found no Teachable/Kajabi showcase evidence for Torah creators, and no gematria or Torah-codes creator with a verifiable product or revenue. Search surfaced only spam-like pages; a Mayim Achronim article notes Bible-codes videos are common on YouTube — [Mayim Achronim](https://mayimachronim.com/?p=1428)
- I could not retrieve prices for Chaim Bentorah's All-Access tier or the FFOZ Torah Club.
- Chabad-adjacent creators and Jewish women's course businesses were not documented with prices.

## Which audience pays more: Jewish or Christian Hebrew-curious buyers?

### Takeaway
The evidence suggests the two audiences pay differently. The Jewish-audience leader (Aleph Beta) earns about 74% of revenue from paid programs. The Christian/Messianic leaders (Fusion Global 86% contributions; FFOZ about 72% contributions) earn mostly from donations, with books and TV extending their reach. Christian Hebrew-curious audiences look larger and fund "ministry" generously. Jewish audiences pay for structured learning subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Aleph Beta FY2025: $2.40M program revenue vs $0.91M contributions — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/273846145)
- Fusion Global FY2024: $2.70M of $3.15M (85.8%) from contributions — [philanthropy.org](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/475619402/fusion-global-inc)
- FFOZ FY2023: about $3.62M contributions vs about $1.37M program revenue, out of about $5.0M — [CauseIQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/first-fruits-of-zion,841233209)
- Hebrew4Christians gives content away free and relies on gifts and book sales — [Hebrew4Christians](https://hebrew4christians.com/About_HFC/Donations/donations.html)
- Christian Hebrew-word books are mass-market products: Sobel through Thomas Nelson and TBN; Bentorah through Whitaker House at $14.99–24.99 — [HarperCollins](https://www.harpercollinschristian.com/9780785240051/mysteries-of-the-messiah); [Christianbook](https://www.christianbook.com/learning-language-workbook-personal-hebrew-study/chaim-bentorah/9781943852932/pd/852933)
- Scale of Christian Hebrew-roots giving: John Hagee Ministries gave $2.1M to Israeli charities — [eJewishPhilanthropy](https://ejewishphilanthropy.com/john-hagee-ministries-gives-2-1m-in-christian-donations-to-israeli-charities/)

### Inferences
- For a mixed and mostly female audience, Christian Hebrew-curious buyers are probably the larger pool for low-ticket items like printables, word-study ebooks and feast guides. Jewish learners are more likely to pay for ongoing parasha subscriptions.
- Product framing matters. Christian buyers respond to "hidden in plain sight" and Messiah-connection framing (Sobel). Jewish buyers respond to text-study rigor (Fohrman). Ink of Sinai's neutral "patterns in the Hebrew text" angle could serve both if it avoids taking a theological side.
- These conclusions rest on 3–4 nonprofit organizations, not on individual creators. Treat them as directional only.

### Gaps
- I found no direct survey or sales data comparing Jewish and Christian buyer spending on Hebrew/Torah digital products.

## Holiday/portion-cycle products (Hanukkah, weekly parasha guides) and how they sell

### Takeaway
Weekly-portion and holiday content is the backbone of every major brand: Aleph Beta's parsha videos and Parsha Lab podcast, FFOZ's Torah Club, and Fusion's holiday teaching. Holidays serve as marketing moments, such as Aleph Beta's Tisha B'Av campaign and Christian feast guides. I found no sales figures for specific holiday products.

### Cited Findings
- Aleph Beta publishes the weekly Parsha Lab podcast and runs holiday-specific campaigns (Tisha B'Av 2026) — [Rephonic](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/parsha-lab-from-aleph-beta); [Jewish Link](https://jewishlink.news/experience-tisha-b-av-with-aleph-beta/)
- Aleph Beta's CEO produced the Maccabeats' Hanukkah video "Candlelight", which has 16M+ views. This is evidence that Hanukkah content can go viral — [faith.tools review](https://faith.tools/app/38-alephbeta)
- Christian feast guides are an established product category. Tyndale's *Feasts of the Bible* participant guide includes a yearly feast calendar; *Celebrating Biblical Feasts* is sold as an instant-download ebook — [Tyndale](https://www.tyndale.com/p/feasts-of-the-bible-participant-guide/22554/e-book); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13422868)
- Fusion Global's teaching covers the significance of the holidays — [philanthropy.org](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/475619402/fusion-global-inc)
- Weekly short parasha posts for women: #PowerParsha — [Rabbi Sacks Legacy](https://rabbisacks.org/people/karen-miller-jackson/)

### Inferences
- A 54-portion annual cycle naturally suits a subscription or a bundle (for example, a yearly "Hidden Patterns in Every Parasha" PDF set). Holiday guides for Hanukkah, Passover and the fall feasts work as seasonal low-ticket products that can be timed to video spikes.
- Feast guides sold to Christians are a proven category with mainstream publishers. A creator version at Etsy or Gumroad prices ($7–25) fits the price benchmarks above.

### Gaps
- I found no unit sales, Etsy sales counts or launch revenue for Hanukkah or parasha guide products. Etsy listing pages showed only view counts.
