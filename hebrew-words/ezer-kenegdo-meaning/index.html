What does “ezer kenegdo” mean?

The Hebrew behind “a helper suitable for him” in Genesis 2:18

By Ink of SinaiPublished November 8, 2026
Short answer

In Genesis 2:18 the woman is called עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ (ezer kenegdo): “a help opposite him,” a help that faces him as his match. The word עֵזֶר appears 21 times in the Hebrew Bible, and in 16 of them the help comes from God. כְּנֶגְדּוֹ appears only twice, both in this story (Genesis 2:18 and 2:20).

The two Hebrew words

עֵזֶרwithout vowels: עזרezer“help,” “helper”
כְּנֶגְדּוֹwithout vowels: כנגדוkenegdo“opposite him,” “corresponding to him”

כְּנֶגְדּוֹ is built on נֶגֶד, “in front of, facing.” The helper stands across from the man, face to face.

The first thing called “not good”

Genesis 1 calls what God made “good” seven times (Genesis 1:4, Genesis 1:10, Genesis 1:12, Genesis 1:18, Genesis 1:21, Genesis 1:25, Genesis 1:31). The first time the Bible says “not good” is Genesis 2:18: לֹא־טוֹב הֱיוֹת הָאָדָם לְבַדּוֹ, “it is not good for the man to be alone.” The answer to that “not good” is the עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ.

Questions people ask

What is the Hebrew word for helper?

עֵזֶר (ezer). It appears 21 times in the Hebrew Bible. Sixteen of them describe help from God.

What does kenegdo mean?

“Opposite him” or “corresponding to him,” from נֶגֶד, “in front of.” It appears only in Genesis 2:18 and Genesis 2:20.

What does “it is not good for man to be alone” mean in Hebrew?

לֹא־טוֹב הֱיוֹת הָאָדָם לְבַדּוֹ: “it is not good, the man being by himself.” It's the first “not good” in the Bible, after seven “good”s in Genesis 1.

How do you pronounce ezer kenegdo?

AY-zer keh-NEG-doh.
