What does “hineni” mean?

“Here I am”: the one-word answer to being called

By Ink of SinaiChecked against the Hebrew textHow we checkPublished November 8, 2026
Short answer

הִנֵּנִי (hineni) means “here I am.” It is הִנֵּה (hinneh, “look, here”) with “me” attached. It's how Abraham, Jacob and Moses answer when a call from heaven repeats their name twice. The fourth person called that way, Samuel, answers differently.

The word

הִנֵּנִיwithout vowels: הנניhineni“here I am”
הִנֵּהwithout vowels: הנהhinneh“look,” “here”

The same letters can also be pointed הִנְנִי, which usually starts a sentence: “here I am, about to…” (Genesis 48:4). As a one-word reply to a call, it is הִנֵּנִי.

The four double calls

Four times in the Hebrew Bible, a call from heaven says a person's name twice.

VerseThe callThe sceneThe answer
Genesis 22:11“Abraham, Abraham”An angel calls from heaven as Abraham raises the knife over Isaacהִנֵּנִי
Genesis 46:2“Jacob, Jacob”God speaks in a night vision before Jacob goes down to Egyptהִנֵּנִי
Exodus 3:4“Moses, Moses”God calls from the burning bushהִנֵּנִי
1 Samuel 3:10“Samuel, Samuel”The fourth call in the nightדַּבֵּר כִּי שֹׁמֵעַ עַבְדֶּךָ
“Speak, for your servant is listening”

Samuel had already said “here I am” four times that night. The first time, he answered the call itself (1 Samuel 3:4). Then three times he ran to Eli: הִנְנִי כִּי־קָרָאתָ לִּי, “here I am, for you called me” (1 Samuel 3:5, 3:6, 3:8). When his name finally comes twice, he stops saying where he is and asks to hear.

Abraham says it three times in one chapter

In Genesis 22, Abraham answers הִנֵּנִי to God at the start (Genesis 22:1), to Isaac on the way up the mountain, הִנֶּנִּי בְנִי, “here I am, my son” (Genesis 22:7), and to the angel at the last moment (Genesis 22:11).

Questions people ask

What does hineni mean?

“Here I am.” It is the word for “here” or “look” with “me” attached, and it is used as a reply when someone is called.

Who said hineni in the Bible?

Abraham (Genesis 22:1), Jacob (Genesis 46:2), Moses (Exodus 3:4), Samuel (1 Samuel 3:4), Isaiah (Isaiah 6:8) and others, listed above.

Why does God call some names twice?

Four times a call from heaven repeats a name: Abraham, Jacob, Moses and Samuel. The text doesn't give a reason.

How do you pronounce hineni?

hee-NAY-nee.
